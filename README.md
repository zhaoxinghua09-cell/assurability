# Assurability

## Definition

**Assurability** — the property of being **insurable**, treated as an *output of governance* rather than an actuarial accident.

Insurance is the oldest governance technology in existence. It does not eliminate risk; it makes risk **priced, pooled, and accountable**. Underwriters have always imposed governance as a condition of cover — fire codes, seaworthiness, aviation airworthiness, cyber hygiene. An autonomous thing that cannot be underwritten is not merely risky; it is **ungoverned**, because no third party will price its failure.

Assurability holds that insurability is **engineered by governance**, and can be tested:

- **Registry (LGD-I)** — the insured object must be *identifiable*: what exactly is covered, at which version, under whose authority.
- **Evidence (LGD-II)** — the risk must be *evidenced*: documented controls, incident history, change records — the underwriting file.
- **Gate (LGD-III)** — cover must be *conditional on gates*: premium and terms adjust to demonstrated gate performance, and cover lapses when gates lapse.

## Why the term was coined

*Governance-Conditioned Insurability* as a descriptive phrase is already occupied — by practitioner writing (basilpuglisi), the *Reinsuring AI* track, and commercial frameworks (Lattice). The descriptive lane is closed.

**Assurability** is a natural English noun that remained **unclaimed as a technical term** — the state of being assurable. It names the *property* rather than the *relationship*. The descriptive subtitle carries search; the coined term carries the claim.
